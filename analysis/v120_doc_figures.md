# PySME v1.2 documentation figures

This note records the provenance and values used in the two v1.2 documentation
figures. The figures are generated by `analysis/v120_doc_figures.py`; no new
synthesis runs are required.

## Figure 1: spectral comparison and runtime

Files:

- `docs/_static/v120/v120_spectral_comparison.png`
- `docs/_static/v120/v120_spectral_comparison.pdf`

Case: cool metal-rich dwarf, plane-parallel, 5195–5205 Å, with the 378,154-line
buffered input list used by the canonical 10 Å release benchmark. This case was
chosen because it is line rich, has the largest end-to-end speed-up in the
canonical matrix, and is not dominated by an intentional historical
weak-line-pruning difference.

Sources:

- scalar timings and array references:
  `analysis/v120_release_benchmark.json`;
- historical spectrum:
  `analysis/v120_release_benchmark_arrays/cool_metal_rich_dwarf_10A__H__00.npz`;
- v1.2 spectrum:
  `analysis/v120_release_benchmark_arrays/cool_metal_rich_dwarf_10A__C1B1__00.npz`.

Values shown:

- historical complete synthesis: `238.6854337499244 s` (`n=1`);
- PySME v1.2 complete synthesis median: `5.994861561979633 s` (`n=4`);
- speed-up: `39.8150034462356x`;
- maximum `|F_v1.2 - F_historical|`: `1.3982156543690394e-9`;
- RMS residual: `6.629265741618207e-10`;
- shared output grid: 201 points from 5195 to 5205 Å.

The figure rounds these values to 238.7 s, 5.995 s, 39.8×,
`1.40e-9`, and `6.63e-10`.

Insertion point: `docs/advance/synthesis_performance.md`, immediately after
the first paragraph of **Numerical behavior and validation**.

Caption:

> Representative 5195–5205 Å comparison for a cool metal-rich dwarf. Complete
> synthesis time decreases from 238.7 s to 5.995 s (39.8×), while the maximum
> normalized-flux difference is 1.40×10^-9. The lower panel shows
> F(v1.2) - F(historical).

## Figure 2: transfer nodes and candidate-line visits

Files:

- `docs/_static/v120/v120_candidate_work_reduction.png`
- `docs/_static/v120/v120_candidate_work_reduction.pdf`

Case: the dedicated generation-batched RKINTS prototype benchmark for the same
cool metal-rich dwarf and 5195–5205 Å line-rich window. Historical and batched
schedulers produced the same physical transfer grid in this case.

Sources:

- transfer-node counts: `analysis/batched_rkints_benchmark.json`;
- interval counters: `analysis/batched_rkints_cool_timing.log`;
- benchmark interpretation: `analysis/batched_rkints_benchmark.md`.

Values shown:

- historical transfer nodes: `10,049`;
- v1.2 batched transfer nodes: `10,049`;
- legacy full-scan candidate equivalent:
  `1,262,274,975 + 1,262,023,776 = 2,524,298,751`;
- indexed candidate visits:
  `4,294,582 + 4,293,746 = 8,588,328`;
- reduction: `99.65977370956597%`, shown as `99.66%`.

The full-scan number is the counter's candidate-work equivalent for the two
adaptive generations; it is not a claim that transfer used fewer physical
wavelength evaluations.

Insertion point: `docs/dev/synthesis_engine.md`, in **Generation-batched
adaptive transfer**, immediately after the explanation of
`LineIntervalSweep`/`OPMTRXIndexed` and preservation of line-list order.

Caption:

> The v1.2 scheduler evaluates the same 10,049 physical transfer wavelengths
> as the historical adaptive calculation in this line-rich test, but
> interval-indexed lookup reduces candidate work from a 2.524-billion full-scan
> equivalent to 8.588 million visits (99.66%).
